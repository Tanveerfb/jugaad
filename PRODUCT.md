## Register

product

## Users

One person — the owner — on their own Windows PC with a 16 GB GPU, in long desk sessions,
dark room by default and daytime too. Works mainly in Node.js and JavaScript; already uses
LM Studio and push-to-talk. Jobs: organise messy folders safely, talk to the app, ask
questions about their own files, audit their projects against `fleet-standards`.

## Product Purpose

A local AI workbench that gets the most out of local models through narrow, reliable
abilities rather than an open-ended agent. Private by default — files and voice never leave
the machine unless the owner allows it per request. Success: a folder organised with every
move approved and undoable, and the GPU never fought over.

## Brand Personality

A *Doctor Who* (modern series, 2012 console room) homage — an obvious one, permitted because
the tool is personal and never distributed. Three words: **inventive, warm, trustworthy**.
Custom shapes and custom motion are wanted (owner, round-1 feedback 2026-10-08: plain
rectangle cards read as generic). Bigger on the inside: compact module tiles that open into
full views.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS dashboards: identical rectangle card grids, hero-metric tiles.
- Cyberpunk (already done on another of the owner's sites).
- A console-style home screen or a glowing orb (both rejected — the owner wants a
  well-organised screen).
- Anything copied from the show: no police-box shape, logos, characters, quotes, or real
  Gallifreyan script.

## Design Principles

1. **The motif lives in signature places** — dashboard, module tiles, the sonic control,
   progress, empty states. File tables, plan reviews and settings stay plain and high-contrast.
2. **Motion conveys state** — scanning, listening, opening a module, applying a plan. Custom
   and memorable, never decoration for its own sake, never making the tool feel slow.
3. **Trust is visible** — nothing moves without approval; every change shows how to undo it.
4. **Inspired, never copied.**

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured everywhere (no exception). State never by
colour alone. Icon-only controls have accessible names.
